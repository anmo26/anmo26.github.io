# things the garden could become

Drawn from what Anmo said he is made of: Murakami, Oda, Monet, the Beatles,
Wong Kar-wai, Shunji Iwai, Yuji Nishida, Cornell, pogo sticking, Johnny Kim,
Conan O'Brien, Elon Musk, Leonardo.

Nothing here is built. This is the list to argue with.

---

## the four worth doing first

### 1. the listening room — everyone hears the same second

Lily Chou-Chou is not a film about music. It is a film about a lot of lonely
people who are all listening to the same thing at the same time and cannot
say so out loud.

The player on this site gives everybody their own private playback, which is
the opposite of that. Instead: one track, one clock, everybody in. You open
the site at 4am and the counter says eleven people are here, and the player
says everyone is thirty-one seconds into the same song. You cannot pause it.
You can only leave.

It needs one shared line of text — what is playing and when it started — and
everybody's player seeks to the same place. The site already has the shared
document and the synchronised clock. This is the single most Lily Chou-Chou
thing the site could do, and no other personal site does it.

### 2. the log pose — the site as a voyage

One Piece is a map problem. You do not browse the Grand Line, you point at
the next island and go.

A needle in the corner that always points at the room you have spent the
least time in. Not a menu, not a sitemap — a direction. Rooms you have never
opened pull harder. It turns twenty-nine folders from a tree into a journey,
and it means a stranger never has to wonder what to click.

### 3. the bounty

Every hand that visits gets a poster: their handle, and a number that goes up
the more of the site they have found — the moon, the crypt, the sea, the
bar, the ether. It is vanity and it is funny and it is exactly One Piece, and
it turns "there are secrets here" from a rumour into a scoreboard.

The site already counts unique visitors per room. This is that data, drawn as
a wanted poster.

### 4. the streak

The site knows when the folder last changed, because the watcher writes it
down. So it knows how many days in a row the garden has been tended.

Put that number on the front page. Not for visitors — for Anmo. A garden that
says "41 days" is a garden that gets watered on day 42. Every discipline
argument he likes comes down to making the streak visible.

## the rest, roughly in order of how much they would change the place

**The second moon** (Murakami). Once in a great while — rarely enough that
people argue about whether it happens — a folder on the front page has a twin
that is not in the real folder. Open it and the room is the same room, very
slightly wrong. Nobody is told. It is 1Q84's second moon: either you looked
up or you did not.

**The expiry date** (Wong Kar-wai). Chungking Express and the pineapple tins.
A note can be given a date to die on. It fades as the date comes and then it
is gone, and the wall stops being an archive and becomes a place where things
are lost. Some things should be allowed to be lost.

**Step printing** (Wong Kar-wai). A paper where motion smears — dragging a
folder leaves a trail of itself, the mini fig blurs when he moves fast. One
more paper in the list, beside monet and bone.

**The chimes** (Cornell). On the hour, a short bell figure, made on the spot
like every other sound here. McGraw tower, the hill, the cold.

**The notebook** (Leonardo). A page written in Anmo's own hand, traced from a
photograph, and mirror-written like the codices — hold it up to a mirror, or
press one key, to read it. The flying machine drawn in ASCII beside it. This
folds in the handwriting-as-a-font idea that is already on the to-do.

**The ornithopter** (Leonardo, and the mini fig). He has a pogo stick, a pair
of legs, and Wukong's cloud. Give him da Vinci's flying machine as a fourth
way to get about — the one that flaps and is not sure it works.

**The height meter** (Nishida). Volleyball is one number: how high. The mini
fig remembers his best jump and there is a faint line across the page where
it was. You spend a while trying to beat it. You will.

**The B-side** (the Beatles). Every page has a second side. Flip the whole
site like a record and the same folder is its own sleeve — the liner notes,
the credits, what was going on the day each thing was made.

**Two minutes of your life** (Conan). A talk show is a person and a desk and
a bit that goes wrong. A page where the site interviews the visitor: three
questions, their answers kept only in their own browser, and the last one is
always stupid.

**The well** (Murakami). A folder you go DOWN into, where there is nothing at
all, and you have to sit in it for a while before anything happens. The crypt
is nearly this and has too much furniture.

---

## what these have in common

Every one of them is a thing the site DOES rather than a thing it shows. The
garden is already unusually alive for a personal website; where it is still
ordinary is that a visitor arrives, looks, and leaves. Each idea above gives
them something to have done.
